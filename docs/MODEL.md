# Remaining-season simulation

This is a small, auditable C++17 research baseline. Complexity does not establish calibration. Compare its held-out proper scores with the uniform and points-per-start baselines before interpreting probability differences. Current-season data alone estimate pace and failures, including in 2026: no pre-regulation constructor strength or driver ranking is carried into the new rules.

## Recent form

For a session with `N` drivers, a rank `r` becomes `z=(N+1-2r)/(N-1)`. GP pace observations include finishers only, ranked among those finishers. Retirements inform reliability separately, avoiding treating a broken car as an intrinsically slow driver. Each GP observation is `z - grid_effect * z_grid`; qualifying observations are `(1-grid_effect) * z_qualifying`. Qualifying therefore contributes evidence about pace, while grid position is added once in future race performance. Lap times from different qualifying stages are not treated as directly comparable.

The recency weight for an observation `a` GP rounds old is `2^(-a/half_life)`, default half-life six rounds. Qualifying gets an additional weight of 0.55. A team mean is the weighted score sum divided by total weight plus 2. A driver's residual relative to that team mean is divided by its total weight plus 4. Predicted underlying form is team mean plus shrunken driver residual. These are descriptive form adjustments: teammates and team strength are confounded, and the components do not identify causal driver skill. A properly identified hierarchical pace model is future work.

Broad manually specified circuit groups (street, fast, mixed) provide small team residual adjustments. The team/group residual uses eight zero-score prior observations and is multiplied by `track_scale` (default 0.15). Circuits outside the explicit street/fast lists use the mixed group. This is a deliberately weak, heuristic characteristic proxy, not a fitted engineering model or a strong claim about a new circuit. Sparse groups shrink toward zero.

The `standings` comparator uses standardized `log(1+points/GP starts)` as performance strength. The `uniform` comparator gives active entrants equal mean strength. Both use common fixed failure rates and no grid or track advantage; historical points and countbacks still determine the championship starting state. An observed future grid remains recorded as available information, but only the form model uses it to change race strength.

## Simulation

Every simulation draws driver and team strength uncertainty once for the remaining season. For the form baseline their standard deviations are `0.025+0.18/sqrt(2+n_eff)`, with `n_eff=(sum weights)^2/sum squared weights`; comparators use 0.08. `parameter_scale` multiplies these draws. This is a heuristic uncertainty distribution, not a fitted posterior or a confidence interval for identified effects. It omits covariance between estimated team and driver components.

Each weekend has a shared team performance shock and a driver shock, each with standard deviation 0.08. These carry through a sprint and GP at the same event. Future qualifying adds Gaussian noise of 0.18 and determines the grid; race performance adds the grid term (default coefficient 0.25) and Gaussian noise of 0.23. `noise_scale` multiplies weekend, qualifying and race noise. Known qualifying supplies an upcoming GP grid without updating the fitted underlying form again. An eligible published starting grid overrides qualifying positions once, including its penalties. No new grid penalties are predicted.

Mechanical risk uses team Beta(1.5,28.5) priors and incident risk uses driver Beta(1,24) priors, updated with recency-weighted observed retirements and starts. Cause attribution is a coarse status-text heuristic. Hazards are drawn once per remaining season. Event-level lognormal team mechanical and common incident multipliers create correlated failures. Sprint failure exposure is 0.35 of a GP. `reliability_scale` multiplies event risks. These defaults and shock sizes are assumptions requiring sensitivity analysis and held-out checking, not calibrated safety probabilities.

Retirees receive a simulated completed distance on a normalized 60-lap GP or 20-lap sprint. Incident retirements have an additional 25% chance of zero completed laps. Cars are ordered by completed distance, then finishers before retirees on equal distance, then simulated pace. This approximates finish-line crossing order; it is not a lap-level race model. Late retirees completing at least `floor(0.9 * winner laps)` can classify and score. Unclassified cars score zero. All nonretirees complete the normalized winner distance; ordinary lapping is not separately modeled. The negligible all-retired case forces the fastest car to finish, consistent with the full-distance condition.

Future races are conditional on full distance taking place. They exclude cancellations, shortened-race points, race dead heats, future substitutions, explicit weather forecasts, safety-car strategy, team orders and unannounced penalties. Observed result points and exact-round standings are used directly, including historically different points rules and penalties; they are never recomputed from modern scoring. An exact-round standings snapshot is preferred for starting championship points, with later observed sprints added. Otherwise, starting points are summed from observed race and sprint rows, with an explicit penalty-coverage warning. Held-out forward simulation uses 2025 folds, when fastest-lap bonus points had already been removed.

## Temporal and roster controls

Only season GP rows at or before `through_round` whose session starts at least 180 minutes before cutoff enter the fit. Qualifying requires start plus 90 minutes and sprint results start plus 60 minutes. These conservative completion windows prevent using a result before an in-progress session would ordinarily finish, but are not independently verified official publication times. Known future qualifying can determine the next grid but cannot enter training form. Published grids additionally require their own availability timestamp at or before cutoff. These controls cannot recover historical revisions to a source; archived original snapshots would be needed for strict point-in-time revision auditing.

All as-of championship entrants retain accrued points, including inactive substitute drivers. Only the latest known GP or later completed qualifying roster races in future sessions. The model holds that roster fixed. Partial or nonunique observed future grids are rejected. Missing qualifying classifications are never imputed; absent sessions and fewer qualifying entrants than GP entrants produce explicit countback-coverage warnings. Nonparticipation may explain some differences.

## Points and ties

Future full-distance GP points are `25,18,15,12,10,8,6,4,2,1`; sprints award `8,7,6,5,4,3,2,1`. No fastest-lap bonus is added. GP countback stores the full classified finishing-position vector; sprint places never enter GP countback. Under 2026 Article A2.1.4(c), tied points compare GP wins, seconds, thirds and every lower position, then the same GP qualifying-result counts. Pre-2026 comparisons stop after GP countback and leave residual ties to FIA discretion. Exact unresolved 2026 ties are randomly allocated simulation ranks and explicitly reported; this computational allocation is not asserted to be a regulatory determination. Missing qualifying history can affect this step.

Sources: [2026 FIA Section A, Issue 03, A2.1.4 and A2.2](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_a_general_provisions_-_iss_03_-_2026-06-25.pdf), and the classification and source details in [SOURCE_REVIEW.md](SOURCE_REVIEW.md).

Outputs include final championship rank distributions, expected total points and 5th/50th/95th percentiles, plus each remaining GP and sprint's finishing-order distributions, expected points and retirement probabilities. Championship top-five membership is the sum of final rank probabilities 1–5; it is not a single-race top-five forecast. Marginal Bernoulli Monte Carlo error is `sqrt(p*(1-p)/simulations)`; it describes numerical simulation noise separately from the much larger model and data uncertainty. Fixed seeds reproduce a run on the same C++ standard-library implementation; random-distribution implementations can differ across platforms.
