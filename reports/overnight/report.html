What helped, what hurt

  • Qualifying helped: removing its pace observations and grid influence worsened boundary Brier in both years.
  • Recency is conditional: removing it improved 2025 but materially worsened 2024. A single favorable year is not enough reason to replace the six-round half-life.
  • Shared team pace evidence is mixed: independent shocks helped 2024 and hurt 2025. This experiment preserves marginal pace variance, rather than gaining confidence by removing noise.
  • Track fit remains unproven: removing it gave tiny Brier improvements in both seeds and seasons, but other scores are mixed. This is a plausible simplification to revisit, not a demonstrated upgrade.
  • Standings remains a serious comparator: it beats form on 2025 rank RPS and on next-race top-five Brier in both seasons. The form model is not uniformly better.

These controls average two runs of 20,000 simulations each at the unchanged cutoff. The pooled Monte Carlo standard errors are about 0.22–0.25 percentage points for these probabilities. That only measures numerical sampling error, not the much larger uncertainty about the model.

Coverage and limits

Existing real Jolpica responses supply 24 GPs and 479 classification rows in each of 2024 and 2025, with 479 qualifying rows in each year. Final standings are used only as evaluation targets; future results, qualifying, grids and standings are removed before fitting. The realized calendar is used retrospectively. The model fixes the latest known roster, so it misses one to three later entrants in some 2024 folds and one in 2025 round 6. Boundary drivers themselves have observed final outcomes.

2024 is secondary evidence. The existing simulator omits future fastest-lap bonus points, which still existed that year. There are 19 observed bonus points in the full 2024 data, with 3–14 across the remaining grid in these folds. Starting points include actual past bonuses, and final targets retain actual bonuses; 2024 point calibration therefore has a known mismatch. No historical rule extension was added for this small pass.

The current snapshot still has 17 completed 2026 GPs and six Sprints, with totals reconciled to standings. Austin qualifying and its provisional penalty-adjusted grid are verified official supplements. The Australian and Spanish 2026 qualifying sources omit some entrants; missing ranks are not imputed, so exact qualifying countback coverage can be incomplete. Later source corrections cannot be reconstructed from retrospective downloads.

Full-distance events, fixed rosters and heuristic pace/reliability scales remain assumptions. Weather forecasts, strategy, future penalties, cancellations and shortened races are absent. The Piastri miss and cross-season reversals show why neither calibration nor future accuracy has been established.
